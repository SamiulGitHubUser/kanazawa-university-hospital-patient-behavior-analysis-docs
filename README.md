# Multimodal Analysis of Post-Surgical Cognitive and Behavioral Conditions

# Research Background

## Overview

This research is a collaborative project between **Japan Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (JAIST)** and **Kanazawa University**, aiming to investigate post-surgical cognitive and behavioral conditions through multimodal analysis of patient interactions.

The study focuses on identifying behavioral differences between patients who exhibit post-operative cognitive or behavioral abnormalities and those who demonstrate normal cognitive and behavioral functioning after surgery. By leveraging video and audio recordings, the project seeks to develop objective methods for assessing patient behavior and engagement in clinical settings.

## Data Collection

Patient data were collected following neurosurgical treatment at Kanazawa University Hospital. All recordings were obtained during post-surgical assessment sessions under approved ethical procedures.

### Participants

A total of **24 patients** participated in the initial study:

- **12 patients** exhibiting behavioral or cognitive abnormalities during post-operative assessment.
- **12 patients** demonstrating normal or active behavior without observable cognitive or behavioral issues.

### Recording Setup

Each participant was recorded using synchronized video and audio modalities during clinical assessment sessions.

The dataset includes:

- Video recordings capturing the patient's upper body.
- Clear visibility of facial regions while participants wore transparent clinical masks.
- High-quality audio recordings of patient speech and interactions.
- Natural patient-clinician communication during assessment tasks.

## Research Objective

The primary goal of this study is to explore whether multimodal behavioral signals can distinguish between patients with abnormal post-operative cognitive or behavioral conditions and those exhibiting normal recovery patterns.

The collected data enable the analysis of:

- Verbal communication characteristics.
- Speech fluency and hesitation patterns.
- Cognitive reaction and response behaviors.
- Non-verbal behavioral cues.
- Patient engagement during clinical tasks.
- Multimodal interactions combining audio and visual information.

Ultimately, this research aims to contribute toward the development of objective, AI-assisted clinical assessment tools that support healthcare professionals in the early detection and monitoring of post-operative cognitive and behavioral impairments.
